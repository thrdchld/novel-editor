# Genre Neutrality

## Scope

This editor standard applies to every genre, subgenre, hybrid genre, and form of fiction. Examples include romance, drama, comedy, thriller, mystery, horror, fantasy, science fiction, crime, historical fiction, slice of life, action, adventure, and combinations or categories not listed here. The examples are not an exhaustive taxonomy.

## Genre is context, not an automatic rule set

- A genre label in metadata or project documentation is descriptive context only.
- Do not infer mandatory tropes, plot formulas, act structures, character archetypes, conflict patterns, pacing, subplot counts, emotional beats, or ending types from a genre label.
- Do not impose a three-act structure, hero's journey, romance arc, happy ending, cliffhanger, mystery reveal, or any other formula unless the author explicitly makes it a project requirement.
- Do not call a story technically broken because it departs from genre convention.
- Do not judge whether the manuscript is sufficiently romantic, funny, frightening, fantastical, realistic, fast-paced, commercial, or marketable during a technical audit.

## When genre-fit review is explicitly requested

If the author asks for genre-fit or brief-compliance review:
1. Use the author's stated target audience, platform, brief, and intended reader promise as the criteria.
2. Do not substitute generic assumptions about the genre for the author's brief.
3. Report genre-fit observations separately from technical findings such as plot holes, continuity errors, timeline contradictions, or manuscript defects.
4. Distinguish a deliberate subversion of convention from an accidental failure to deliver a promised element. Ask for clarification when intent cannot be established.
5. Never rewrite the story to satisfy a convention the author did not request.

## Technical standards remain universal

Regardless of genre, audit internal causality, timeline, continuity, character knowledge, world/system consistency, setup/payoff where relevant, reader comprehension, and objective manuscript correctness. Apply each test only where the manuscript contains the relevant element. For example, audit magic-system limits only when magic exists; do not require magic, romance, mystery, or any other genre component to exist.

## Configuration

project.genre_context may describe the project's genre for orientation, terminology, or an explicitly requested genre-fit review. It must never activate mandatory conventions or change the universal technical audit by itself.
